# Consumables planning

Food, water and fuel are the only mass that changes during a trip, so these are where a
plan is made to fit — and where it is most often wrong.

## Energy

| Exertion band | kcal/person/day | Typical case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sedentary basecamp | 2,500 | Fixed camp, short walks, warm |
| Moderate hiking | 3,000–3,500 | 15–20 km/day, 20–25 kg pack, temperate |
| Cold weather or heavy load | 4,000–5,000 | Below freezing, snow travel, 25 kg+, long days |

Cold is the multiplier people forget: thermoregulation adds 500–1,000 kcal/day before
any extra distance is walked.

## Food weight

Plan **700–900 g/person/day of dry weight**, excluding packaging. What decides where you
land in that range is calorie density:

| Ration density | To hit 3,200 kcal |
| --- | --- |
| 4.0 kcal/g — ordinary mixed hill food | 800 g/day |
| 4.5 kcal/g — deliberately fat-forward | 710 g/day |

Fat carries 9 kcal/g against 4 for carbohydrate and protein: 100 ml of olive oil adds
~830 kcal for 92 g. The cheapest weight saving on any trip, and routinely ignored.

## The rationing reserve

**Always carry one extra day, at roughly 60% ration.**

- 1,800–2,000 kcal per person
- 400–500 g per person
- No cooking required — it must work with a dead stove
- Sealed, carried whole by one nominated person, not split across packs
- Not snack food. If it is accessible it will be eaten on day two

It exists for a delay, not for a change of plan.

## Water

| Conditions | Litres/person/day |
| --- | --- |
| Temperate, moderate exertion | 3–4 (2.5–3 drinking, 0.5–1 cooking and cleaning) |
| Hot or arid, high exertion | 5–8, and heat casualties start with people who rationed |
| Cold, dry air | 3.5–4.5 — thirst is suppressed and dehydration is common |
| Snow-melting camp | Same intake, very different fuel bill |

What matters more than the daily figure is the **carry between resupply points**. Map
the sources, find the longest dry stretch, size bottles for that stretch plus one litre.
Water is 1 kg/L — a spare three litres "in case" is an extra tent.

## Treatment throughput

| Method | Throughput | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squeeze / inline hollow-fibre filter | 1.5–2 L/min new, halves as it clogs | Backflush daily. One freeze destroys it — it sleeps inside your bag |
| Gravity filter | 4–6 L in 5–10 min, hands-free | The right default for groups of four or more |
| Pump filter | ~1 L/min, and it is work | Handles shallow or silty sources nothing else can |
| Chlorine dioxide tablets or drops | 30 min bacteria and viruses, **4 hours** for Cryptosporidium | No particulate removal. Excellent as a lightweight backup |
| UV pen | 90 s/L | Needs clear water and a live battery |
| Boiling | 1 min at a rolling boil, 3 min above 2,000 m | Costs about 16 g of gas per litre. A backup, not a primary |

Chemical treatment is the correct backup for a filter because it fails differently: a
second identical filter is not a backup, since the same silt clogs both.

## Fuel

| Conditions | Canister gas |
| --- | --- |
| Temperate, two hot drinks and two hot meals | 15–25 g/person/day |
| Below freezing, windy, no shelter for the stove | 30–50 g/person/day |
| Melting snow for all water | 80–150 g/person/day |

A "230 g" canister weighs about 370 g full and 140 g empty — plan on net gas, carry
gross weight. Liquid fuel runs 90–120 ml/person/day when melting snow.

Round up to whole canisters. A part-used canister plus a full spare is the standard
configuration, and you cannot know what is left in a part-used one without weighing it.

## Worked example: four people, three days, temperate, moderate hiking

**Roster and bands**

| Person | Body weight | Conditioning | Band | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 92 kg | Conditioned, load-experienced | 28% | 25.8 kg |
| B | 78 kg | Typical | 20% | 15.6 kg |
| C | 64 kg | Typical | 20% | 12.8 kg |
| D | 58 kg | Unconditioned, first multi-day trip | 15% | 8.7 kg |

Total allowance: **62.9 kg**.

**Consumables**
- Energy: 3,200 kcal/person/day at 4.0 kcal/g → 800 g/person/day
- Food: 3 days × 800 g = 2.40 kg each, plus 0.45 kg reserve = **2.85 kg each, 11.4 kg group**
- Fuel: 12 person-days × 20 g = 240 g net → one 230 g and one 100 g canister, **0.57 kg gross**
- Water: sources every 6–8 km, so 1.5 L carried each = **1.5 kg each**

**Group hardware**

| Item | kg | Item | kg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 × two-person tents | 4.80 | Repair kit | 0.35 |
| Stove, pot set, backup stove | 0.60 | Maps, two compasses, GPS | 0.45 |
| Fuel (gross) | 0.57 | Satellite messenger | 0.11 |
| Filter, gravity bag, tablets | 0.33 | Trowel and waste kit | 0.10 |
| Group first aid | 0.55 | **Hardware total** | **7.86** |

Group mass to distribute: 7.86 + 11.4 = **19.3 kg**.

**The naive even split fails**

Personal base kit: A 9.0, B 8.5, C 7.5, D 8.0 kg. Split group mass four ways at
4.82 kg each and add 1.5 kg of water:

| Person | Load | Band | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 15.3 | 25.8 | 59% — badly under-used |
| B | 14.8 | 15.6 | 95% |
| C | 13.8 | 12.8 | **over by 1.0 kg** |
| D | 14.3 | 8.7 | **over by 5.6 kg** |

Total load 58.2 kg against 62.9 kg of allowance: it fits in aggregate and does not fit
as split. That is the normal result, and the reason the ledger exists.

**The binding constraint:** D's band is 8.7 kg and D's personal kit plus water is 9.5 kg — over before a gram of
group mass is added. No rebalance of group gear can fix that; the constraint binds on
D's own kit, and the honest finding is exactly that sentence.

**The re-split.** Cut D's personal base to 6.5 kg (borrowed lighter pack and bag), give D 1.0 L of water
and no group mass, then fill the other three towards their bands:

| Person | Personal | Water | Group | Total | Band | % |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 9.0 | 1.5 | 12.0 | 22.5 | 25.8 | 87% |
| B | 8.5 | 1.5 | 4.3 | 14.3 | 15.6 | 92% |
| C | 7.5 | 1.5 | 3.0 | 12.0 | 12.8 | 94% |
| D | 6.5 | 1.0 | 0.0 | 7.5 | 8.7 | 86% |

Group mass 19.3 kg placed, every band respected, and A carries the tents, fuel and food
because A can.

**Headroom:** nobody should start above about 90–95% of their band. C at 94% has 0.8 kg of slack,
which one forgotten item consumes. Consumables burn down at roughly 1–1.5 kg/person/day,
so the second morning is comfortable — say that, because it changes whether 94% is
acceptable.
