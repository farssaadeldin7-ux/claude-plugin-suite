# Symptom to root cause

The player names an outcome. You need a cause. This table is genre-general — it works
on the structure of the mistake, not on any game's specifics.

Read each row as: the complaint, what actually produces it, and the **one question**
that eliminates the most candidates. Ask the discriminating questions, not all of them.

## "I keep dying"

The most common complaint and the least informative. Four causes, four different sheets.

| Root cause | What it looks like | Discriminating question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Positioning** | Dies first in fights, before doing damage. Deaths cluster at fight start. | "In the fights you lose, are you dying early or late?" |
| **Cooldown discipline** | Dies with defensive abilities on cooldown, spent offensively a moment earlier | "When you die, is your escape or defensive ability available?" |
| **Map awareness** | Dies alone, to numbers, away from the team. Deaths cluster in the same places. | "Are you usually dying in a fight, or on your own?" |
| **Overextending on losing tempo** | Deaths follow a lost objective, a lost round or a teammate's death | "What had just happened, the ten seconds before you died?" |

The tempo cause is the one players almost never self-diagnose. If deaths follow losses
rather than causing them, the sheet is about disengaging after a loss, not positioning.

## "I lose the early game"

| Root cause | What it looks like | Discriminating question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opener knowledge** | Behind before the first fight. Nothing went wrong; they were simply slower. | "Are you behind before anything happens, or after the first fight?" |
| **Mechanics under pressure** | Executes the opener fine in practice, drops it when contested | "Can you do it cleanly when nobody is contesting you?" |
| **Risk tolerance** | Takes early fights or greedy lines with no information | "How often does your early game end because you chose a fight?" |

These three need opposite sheets. Opener knowledge is a memorisation problem — cheap to
fix, Cost 1–2, huge yield. Mechanics is a reps problem — Cost 4–5, one per session.
Risk tolerance is a decision problem — Cost 1, and usually the highest yield of the
three. Check risk tolerance first.

## "I plateau" / "I'm stuck at this rank"

Almost never an execution problem. Players who have arrived at a plateau usually have
adequate mechanics for one rank above it.

| Root cause | What it looks like | Discriminating question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision quality** | Individual actions are clean; the choice of which action was wrong | "In your losses, do you lose fights you should win, or win fights that did not matter?" |
| **Narrow pool** | Fine on two characters, unplayable otherwise; loses to counter-picks | "How many games do you start already at a disadvantage?" |
| **Playing to not lose** | Passive, low variance, never converts an advantage into a win | "When you are ahead, what do you do differently?" |
| **Session structure** | Wins early in a session, loses it back over the next four games | "Where in your session are your losses?" |

Winning fights that did not matter is the signature plateau failure. It feels like
playing well and does not move rating. Diagnose it by asking what the fight was for.

## "I win my lane / round / early game but lose the match"

One cause, three flavours: an advantage acquired is not being converted. Ask **"what do
you do in the two minutes after you win?"** The answers separate cleanly.

- "Look for the next fight" — no conversion plan; the sheet is about objectives
- "Play safe so I don't lose it" — hoarding a decaying resource
- "I don't know" — the honest and most common answer; the sheet is a conversion checklist

## "I do no damage" / "I feel useless"

| Root cause | Discriminating question |
| --- | --- |
| Target selection — attacking what is hard to kill rather than what matters | "Who are you usually attacking first?" |
| Uptime — alive and present, but not engaged; damage windows missed | "What fraction of a fight are you actually attacking?" |
| Role misread — doing the job the character is not for | "What do you think your job is in a fight?" |

## "I'm inconsistent"

| Root cause | Discriminating question |
| --- | --- |
| Adapting to the game rather than running a plan | "Do you decide your plan before the match or during it?" |
| Fatigue — quality falls off a cliff after N games | "Which game of a session is your worst?" |
| Genuine matchup variance, not inconsistency | "Are your bad games against particular opponents?" |

## "I tilt"

Do not write a tactical sheet for a tilt problem. It will not be read. The changes go
on session structure instead: a hard game cap, a stop rule after two consecutive losses,
one physical reset between games. Say plainly that this is what the sheet is about.

## Rules for using this table

1. **Ask at most three questions.** Pick the ones that separate, not the ones that are
   interesting.
2. **Take contradictions seriously.** If their answers point at two causes, the sheet
   goes to the one with the cheaper fix — a Cost 1 decision change tested for one
   session is better evidence than more questions.
3. **The player's own diagnosis is data, not the answer.** Someone who says "I know my
   positioning is bad" has usually diagnosed the symptom they can see. Test it against
   the discriminating question anyway.
4. **If nothing fits, say so.** Ask for one specific recent loss, described in order:
   what happened, what they chose, and what happened next. That narrative resolves more
   cases than another round of questions.
