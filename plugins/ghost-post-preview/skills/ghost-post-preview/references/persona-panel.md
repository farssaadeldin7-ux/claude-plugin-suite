# The persona panel

## What this is, and what it is not

These personas are **stated assumptions**. They are not the user's audience, they are not
research, and no one has been surveyed. They are five recurring reader types that account
for most of the ways a post fails, written down so the re-read is systematic rather than
whatever occurred to you first.

The method's honesty rests entirely on saying this out loud. State it in the output. If
the user treats the panel as evidence about their real followers, correct them.

Where a persona's reaction depends on something you do not know — whether their audience
is technical, whether these people have bought before, whether the author has standing in
this space — say the panel cannot answer it rather than inventing a reaction.

## 1. The skeptical scroller

**Default state:** moving, not reading. Gives the post about half a second.

| Notices | Bounces at |
| --- | --- |
| Line one, and only line one | Any word that signals "this is content marketing" |
| Whether it looks like an ad | A first line that could top any post |
| Shape on screen — wall of text or not | Preamble of any kind |
| Whether a real person wrote it | Phrases like "excited to share", "thrilled to announce", "in today's landscape" |

This persona is the majority of impressions. If they bounce, nothing else in the panel
matters, and their verdict is largely the fold test's verdict.

The useful output: quote the exact word at which they stop.

## 2. The in-market buyer

**Default state:** has the problem right now, is quietly evaluating.

| Notices | Bounces at |
| --- | --- |
| Whether the author has actually done this | Claims with no specifics behind them |
| Numbers, timescales, named constraints | Advice that would apply to any company |
| Whether there is a next step, and how pushy it is | A hard sell in a post that promised insight |
| Evidence the author understands their situation | Any detail that reveals a mismatch in scale or sector |

This is the smallest slice of the audience and usually the whole commercial point of the
post. A post that pleases the scroller and gives this reader nothing to act on is a
reach success and a business failure — say so when the stated goal is leads.

The useful output: what this reader would need in order to make contact, and whether the
draft contains it.

## 3. The peer or competitor

**Default state:** reads carefully, uncharitably, and to the end.

| Notices | Bounces at |
| --- | --- |
| Overclaims and rounded numbers | A claim that contradicts something they know |
| Whether the insight is actually new | Repackaged commonplaces presented as discovery |
| Credit that should have been given | Borrowed framing without attribution |
| The gap between the story told and how the industry works | Survivorship bias presented as method |

This persona rarely engages, but they set the author's professional reputation. Their
value is as a fact-check: anything this reader would raise an eyebrow at is worth flagging
before publication, because it will be raised eventually.

The useful output: the one sentence a knowledgeable reader would push back on.

## 4. The algorithm as reader

**Default state:** not a reader at all. Included as a persona because the ranking system
is a gatekeeper with legible preferences, and treating it as an audience member is a
useful fiction.

| Notices | Downranks |
| --- | --- |
| Dwell time, expands, and how fast they arrive | Outbound links in the body, on most platforms |
| Replies and their depth, over likes | Engagement bait it can pattern-match |
| Saves and sends where those exist | Content it cannot classify |
| Early velocity in the first 30-60 minutes | Reposts and cross-platform watermarks |

Read the draft against the correct row of `platform-mechanics.md`, not against a general
sense of what algorithms like. Be honest that this is a model of a system nobody outside
the platform can see, and that it changes.

The useful output: the one mechanical change that would help distribution, stated
separately from the editorial changes.

## 5. The hostile quote-tweeter

**Default state:** looking for something to react to in front of their own audience.

| Notices | Seizes on |
| --- | --- |
| The most extreme sentence in the post | Sweeping generalisations |
| Anything that reads as self-congratulation | Advice from a position of unacknowledged advantage |
| A claim with an obvious counterexample | "Nobody talks about" claims about things widely discussed |
| Tone mismatch with the subject | A business lesson drawn from someone else's hardship |

Most relevant on X and Reddit, real but slower on LinkedIn, largely absent on Instagram
and TikTok where the equivalent is the comment section.

Screenshot risk is worth naming separately. Ask: if one sentence of this post were
screenshotted with no context, which one would it be, and does it survive the crop?

The useful output: the exact sentence most likely to be quoted against the author, and
whether the reach is worth it.

## Optional sixth persona

Add one when the user's audience has a defining characteristic the five above miss — a
regulated industry, an existing customer base reading as customers, a technical audience
that will check the code, a local community, a hiring manager. Define it in one line,
state that you have invented it for this post, and read as it once.

Do not add more than one. Six voices is already more than a reader can hold, and the
panel exists to find one failure, not to generate a list.

## Reading the panel

The panel is not a vote. Weight it by the stated goal:

| Goal | Persona that governs |
| --- | --- |
| Reach | Skeptical scroller, then the algorithm |
| Replies and discussion | Peer or competitor, then the hostile quote-tweeter |
| Leads, DMs, sales | In-market buyer, decisively |
| Saves and shares | Skeptical scroller for the stop, in-market buyer for the utility |
| Reputation and standing | Peer or competitor, then the hostile quote-tweeter |

If the personas disagree, that is signal rather than noise: it usually means the post is
trying to serve two goals. Say which one it should pick.
