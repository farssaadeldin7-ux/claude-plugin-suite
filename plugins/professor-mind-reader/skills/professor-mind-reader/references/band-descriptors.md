# Band descriptors

What actually separates one band from the next, and how the names map across systems.

Use this to position a draft as a **range**, never as a number. You have not seen the
cohort, the marker or the moderation meeting.

## Naming, mapped

| UK undergraduate | UK % | US letter | US GPA | ECTS | Plain name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First (1st) | 70-100 | A / A+ | 4.0 | A | Excellent |
| Upper second (2:1) | 60-69 | B+ / A- | 3.3-3.7 | B | Good |
| Lower second (2:2) | 50-59 | B / B- | 2.7-3.0 | C | Satisfactory |
| Third | 40-49 | C / C+ | 2.0-2.3 | D/E | Adequate |
| Fail | 0-39 | D / F | 0-1.0 | F | Fail |

Two cautions. UK 70% and US "A" are not the same object: a UK first is a band that runs
to 100 and is rarely awarded above 85, while a US A commonly means 93-100. And a UK
mark of 65 is a solid grade, not the near-fail an American reader hears. Where an
institution publishes its own descriptors, those override this table.

Postgraduate UK typically shifts the boundaries up: 70+ distinction, 60-69 merit, 50-59
pass, below 50 fail. Check the handbook.

## What actually separates the bands

| Band | Evidence use | Argument | Sources | Judgement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fail | Absent, wrong, or not on topic | No thread | None, or uncited | None |
| Third | Present but descriptive | Assertion without support | Few, mostly lecture slides | None |
| 2:2 | Accurate, summarised source by source | A position stated, unevenly supported | Set reading, used as summary | Occasional, unsupported |
| 2:1 | Accurate, relevant, well organised | A clear position sustained throughout | Set reading plus some independent, used correctly | Present but borrowed — reports what critics say |
| 1st | Selected, weighed, some sources rejected on stated grounds | A position the student owns, with the strongest objection answered | Independent reading, including material against the position | **The student's own, defended** |

## The 2:1 to 1st pivot

This is the finding to put in front of the student, because they almost always guess
wrong about it. Asked what a first needs, students say "more research", "more sources",
"more content". It is almost never that.

**The difference is evaluation and independent judgement.**

A 2:1 draft demonstrates that the student has understood the field. A first demonstrates
that the student has a defensible position within it and can hold it against the best
argument on the other side.

Three concrete moves, in order of return:

1. **Adjudicate.** Wherever two sources are cited near each other, add a sentence saying
   which is more persuasive here and on what grounds. Two or three of these across an
   essay is often the entire gap.
2. **Steelman then answer.** Find the strongest argument against the position — not a
   weak one that is easy to dismiss — state it fairly, then say why the position
   survives it. Markers reward this because it is the hardest thing to fake.
3. **Show selection.** Say why a source was chosen, or why a widely cited one is not
   used. This signals that reading was directed rather than accumulated.

What does **not** move a 2:1 to a first:

- More sources. A draft with 14 references and no judgement stays a 2:1.
- More length. Beyond the word count, length is a penalty.
- Better prose. Fluency lifts a 2:2 to a 2:1; it does not lift a 2:1 to a first.
- More theory explained. That is criterion-A work, and criterion A is usually cheap.

## Reading published descriptors

Institutional descriptors use a small vocabulary that maps onto the verb ladder in
`rubric-decomposition.md`. Translate before judging:

| Phrase in the descriptor | What it demands |
| --- | --- |
| "comprehensive understanding" | Coverage. Rung 1-2. Cheap. |
| "systematic" | Structure and completeness, not insight |
| "critical" | Rung 6. Judgement with stated criteria. |
| "sophisticated" / "nuanced" | Conditions and exceptions named — "X holds, except where Y" |
| "originality" / "independent thought" | Rung 6-7. A position, or a synthesis, that is the student's |
| "authoritative" | Sources beyond the reading list, used confidently |
| "publishable quality" | Realistically: a strong first. Do not treat literally. |
| "some evidence of" | The band below the one where the phrase is absent |

The word "critical" appearing anywhere in the top band and not in the band below is the
clearest signal a descriptor set can give you: evaluation is the boundary.

## Honesty rules for band positioning

- Give a range spanning at least one band boundary when the draft is near a boundary.
- Name what is holding it at the lower band, as a specific missing observable.
- Never state or imply a percentage.
- Say explicitly that this is a reading of published descriptors, not a prediction of the
  mark, and that markers and moderation vary.
- If no band descriptors were supplied, say the positioning is generic and weaker for it.
