# Arc scoring and the Q&A ledger

How to score a cut, how to keep the question ledger, and a worked 12-scene example.

Scoring is an act of reading, not measurement. Two experienced editors scoring the same
cut will land within about one point of each other on most scenes and disagree sharply on
a few. That is expected and it is fine — the analysis depends on the *shape* of the
series, not on the absolute value of any single scene.

## Scene boundaries

A new scene starts at any of: a change of location, a change of time, a change of
speaker where the speaker becomes the subject, or a change of function (exposition to
testimony, testimony to observation). A cutaway inside a continuous interview is not a
new scene.

Aim for 8 to 30 scenes. Fewer than 8 and the derivative is meaningless. More than 30 and
you are scoring shots.

## Valence, −3 to +3

Valence is the emotional charge of the scene as cut, from the viewer's seat — not the
subject's mood and not the film-maker's sympathy.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| +3 | Release, triumph, reunion, the thing they wanted |
| +2 | Warmth, competence, progress that is clearly progress |
| +1 | Mild positive; things are working |
| 0 | Neutral, procedural, expository, observational |
| −1 | Unease, friction, mild setback |
| −2 | Loss, failure, betrayal, real damage |
| −3 | Devastation, the worst thing in the film |

Score 0 honestly. A film full of ±1 scores is usually a scorer who is reluctant to call
anything neutral, and it hides monotony rather than revealing it.

## Intensity, 0 to 5

Intensity is how much is being demanded of the viewer: emotional load, information
density, pace of cutting, or all three at once.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Wallpaper. Establishing, transitional, breathing |
| 1 | Low. Ambient observation, gentle context |
| 2 | Steady. Ordinary conversational testimony |
| 3 | Engaged. Something is at stake and the viewer knows it |
| 4 | High. Confrontation, revelation, dense information under pressure |
| 5 | Peak. Once or twice per film, three times in a feature at most |

More than three 5s means the scale has drifted. Rescore rather than argue with the flags
that result.

## Reading the series

Look at the differences between consecutive scenes, not the values.

| Pattern | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Valence flat for five minutes | Tonal monotony, whatever the level |
| Intensity flat at 3–4 for a long stretch | Fatigue; the viewer cannot tell what matters |
| Intensity 5 not followed by a drop to 1 or below | No landing. The peak is wasted |
| Valence swinging ±3 every scene | Whiplash; nothing accumulates |
| Rising intensity across the last quarter | Correct for nearly every form |

The single most useful derived figure is the largest gap between valence changes of two
or more points. In a film of any length, if that gap exceeds five minutes, look there
first.

## The question-and-answer ledger

One row per question. Reproduce it in full in the output.

| # | Question | Opens | Closes | How it closes | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Will the yard reopen? | 00:15 | 09:45 | On screen, explicit | Central |
| Q2 | Why did Dan stay? | 00:40 | 07:00 | Implied, in testimony | Major |
| Q3 | What is in the ledger book? | 05:50 | — | Never closed | Minor |

**Weight** is Central, Major or Minor. Exactly one question is Central. If you cannot
identify a single Central question, that is the finding — say so before anything else,
because a film with two central questions and no hierarchy will read as two films.

**How it closes** matters: "on screen, explicit" is strongest, "implied, in testimony"
is acceptable, "closed off-screen" and "never closed" both need flagging. An unclosed
Minor question is often fine and occasionally deliberate. An unclosed Central question
at the end credits is the whole note.

## Worked example — 12-scene short doc, 11:40

Subject: a boatyard facing closure. Form: short doc. No retention data supplied.

| # | Timecode | Scene | Dur | Val | Int | Opens | Closes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 00:00 | Dawn, empty yard, radio news of the sale | 0:35 | −1 | 2 | Q1 | — |
| 2 | 00:35 | Dan opens up, says he has worked here 31 years | 0:50 | 0 | 2 | Q2 | — |
| 3 | 01:25 | History of the yard, archive, narration | 1:40 | 0 | 2 | — | — |
| 4 | 03:05 | The economics of small boatbuilding, expert | 1:20 | 0 | 2 | — | — |
| 5 | 04:25 | Council planning process explained | 1:10 | 0 | 2 | — | — |
| 6 | 05:35 | Dan finds the old ledger book | 0:45 | +1 | 3 | Q3 | — |
| 7 | 06:20 | Dan on his father, unbroken, single frame | 1:50 | −2 | 4 | — | Q2 |
| 8 | 08:10 | The buyer's letter arrives | 0:40 | −2 | 4 | — | — |
| 9 | 08:50 | Meeting in the shed, argument | 0:55 | −1 | 4 | — | — |
| 10 | 09:45 | The vote goes their way | 0:35 | +3 | 5 | — | Q1 |
| 11 | 10:20 | Celebration in the yard | 0:50 | +2 | 3 | — | — |
| 12 | 11:10 | Dawn again, boat launches, credits | 0:30 | +2 | 2 | — | — |

**What the table says.**

Scenes 3, 4 and 5 are three consecutive information-only scenes running 01:25 to 05:35 —
**no question open**, tripped, 4:10 of run-time exposed. Valence sits at exactly 0 from
00:35 to 05:35, five minutes to the second — **tonal monotony**, tripped, and it sits
precisely where a short doc's audience leaves.

Scene 7 runs 1:50 unbroken on a single frame — **texture starvation**, tripped at more
than three times the 30-second limit for the form. It is also the strongest scene in the
film, which is exactly why the note is a reframe at around 07:00 rather than a cut.

Q1 closes at 09:45 of 11:40, ratio 0.84 — within the 70–85% window for a short doc. No
premature resolution. Q3 never closes; Minor, and the ledger book pays off visually in
scene 12, so leave it.

Two more trip on the same material. Scenes 3–5 keep the subject a topic rather than a
person for 4:10 — **stakes not personalised**, tripped, overlapping the no-question
stretch and raising cut #1's priority. And scene 10 peaks at intensity 5 with the scenes
after it at 3 and 2, never dropping to 1 — **no landing** by the pattern table above;
the celebration needs a beat of stillness before the credits.

**Three highest-leverage cuts.**

| Rank | Change | Effort | Exposed run-time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cut scenes 3–5 to 2:00 total and lift Dan's father material from 7 into the gap | 2 | 4:10 |
| 2 | Reframe or cutaway at 07:00 inside scene 7 | 1 | 1:50 |
| 3 | Move the ledger-book discovery (6) before the history block | 2 | 0:45 |

Exposed run-time is run-time currently sitting under a flag. It is not a prediction of
watch time gained, and this analysis does not produce one.
