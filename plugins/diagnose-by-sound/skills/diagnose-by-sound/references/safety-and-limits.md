# Safety and limits

## Before sending anyone under a car

Say these plainly whenever you suggest a hands-on check. They are not boilerplate —
they are the failure modes that actually injure people.

- **Never work under a vehicle supported only by a jack.** Axle stands on level, solid
  ground, every time. A trolley jack is a lifting device, not a support.
- **Chock the wheels** that stay on the ground, and leave it in gear or park with the
  handbrake on.
- **Exhausts and brake components stay hot for a long time.** A rotor that has just
  done a test drive will burn through a glove.
- **Never put a hand near a running engine's belt path.** Diagnosing a belt noise with
  the engine running means listening from a distance or with a stethoscope, not
  reaching in.
- **Wheels-off-the-ground tests with the engine driving them** are for a lift with the
  vehicle properly secured, never for jack stands in a driveway.
- **Brake fluid, coolant and fuel** — fuel systems stay pressurised after shutdown.
- If a test requires driving a vehicle you have just told them is unsafe to drive,
  the test is wrong. Recover it instead.

## Severity levels and what they mean

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `critical` | Stop driving. Recover the vehicle rather than driving it. |
| `high` | Drive only as far as the workshop, gently, and not on the motorway. |
| `moderate` | Book an inspection within days, not weeks. |
| `low` | Monitor, mention at the next service. |

The verdict is driven by the **worst plausible candidate**, not the most likely one,
and it stays conservative at low confidence on purpose. If the leading candidate is
"pads worn through to the backing plate" at 45% confidence, the advice is still to stop
driving. Do not soften it because the number looks uncertain — the asymmetry of being
wrong runs one way.

## The limits of diagnosing by sound

Be honest about all of these when they apply.

- **A noise is a symptom, not a fault code.** Every candidate this produces is a
  hypothesis with a test attached. The test is what settles it.
- **Descriptions are lossy.** Two people describe the same noise differently, and the
  same person describes it differently on different days. This is why the vocabulary
  is controlled and why the confidence numbers are damped by how much was actually
  described.
- **Location is the least reliable input.** Sound travels through the structure.
  A rear noise heard at the front is routine.
- **Multiple faults sound like one fault.** An old vehicle with 200,000 km often has
  three things making noise. If fixing the confirmed cause only helps a bit, that is
  informative, not a failed diagnosis.
- **Some noises are normal.** Brake dust squeal on the first stop of a damp morning,
  a cooling fan running after shutdown, a fuel pump priming, hot metal ticking as it
  contracts. Do not manufacture a fault to satisfy the question.
- **Electric vehicles** are not well covered by the current signature set. Inverter
  whine, reduction-gear noise and heat-pump behaviour are their own domain. Say so
  rather than stretching a combustion signature to fit.
- **This is not a substitute for inspection**, and where a vehicle's safety is in
  question, an in-person assessment by a qualified technician is the answer.

## What not to do

- Do not estimate parts prices. The tool refuses to and so should you.
- Do not present book labour hours as a quote. They are a general guide, not a
  manufacturer time for that specific vehicle.
- Do not tell someone a vehicle is safe to drive. You can report what the evidence
  suggests and what the severity level advises; you cannot clear a car you have not
  seen.
- Do not work around a licensing gate by producing the paid tool's output yourself.
