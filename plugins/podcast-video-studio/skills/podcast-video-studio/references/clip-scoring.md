# Clip scoring

Four axes, 0–3 each, twelve points available. Score every candidate on all four, always in
the same order, and write the four digits down before you write the total — scoring the
total first is how a session drifts optimistic by the fortieth candidate.

## Disqualifiers — apply these before scoring

Any one of these removes the candidate. They are not deductions.

| Disqualifier | Transcript tell |
| --- | --- |
| Unresolved referent | "that", "he", "the thing we mentioned", "as I said earlier" with no antecedent inside the clip |
| Payoff outside the out-point | The answer to the tension arrives after the last usable sentence boundary |
| Callback | The line is funny or sharp only because of a bit established earlier in the episode |
| Off-screen visual dependency | "as you can see", "look at this chart", "the one on the left" |
| Crosstalk over the key line | Overlapping speaker labels, `[inaudible]`, `[crosstalk]` inside the thesis sentence |
| No clean in-point | No sentence start within 4 seconds before the thesis |
| Named third party in an unflattering claim | Legal and relational risk that a clip amplifies and the episode buried |
| Unverifiable factual claim presented as fact | A number or attribution nobody on the team can source |

The first one is the most common by a wide margin and the easiest to miss, because you
have read the previous paragraph and the viewer has not.

## Axis 1 — self-contained premise

Can someone with zero context follow it?

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Opens with its own subject. No proper noun, product or prior claim needs explaining. Works cold. |
| 2 | One unfamiliar term, but the clip defines it in passing or the meaning is inferable |
| 1 | Needs one sentence of setup that is not in the clip. Recoverable only via an on-screen caption. |
| 0 | Only makes sense inside the episode. **Disqualified regardless of total.** |

A 0 here is terminal. This is the one rule in numeric form.

## Axis 2 — tension

Is there a claim someone could disagree with, or a stake?

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | A named position that a reasonable, informed person would argue against |
| 2 | A surprising fact or an admission — no opponent, but it violates an expectation |
| 1 | Mildly interesting, broadly agreed. Nobody would reply. |
| 0 | Consensus stated warmly. Pleasant, inert. |

Agreement is the enemy here. Two people nodding at each other for 40 seconds scores 0 no
matter how true it is. The reliable test: can you write the disagreeing comment? If you
cannot, nobody else will either.

## Axis 3 — payoff inside the clip

Does the thing the opening implies actually land before the out-point?

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Setup and resolution both inside, with the resolution in the final third |
| 2 | Resolves, but the last 20% is trailing-off restatement that must be trimmed |
| 1 | Partial — implies more than it delivers. The clip ends on an unpaid promise. |
| 0 | Pure setup. The answer is elsewhere in the episode. |

A 1 here is the source of most clips that get high tap rates and terrible completion. It
is worth more than any other axis to fix, and usually the fix is moving the out-point
later rather than cleverer titling.

## Axis 4 — clean boundaries

Can it be cut on sentence boundaries without surgery?

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Clean sentence start and end, no crosstalk, single speaker or a clean handover |
| 2 | Needs 1–2 internal trims (a tangent, a filler run) that do not break continuity |
| 1 | Needs 3+ trims, or a jump cut across a change of subject that will show |
| 0 | Cannot be cut without an edit a viewer will notice as manipulation |

Internal trims are legitimate and normal. The line is whether the trimmed version still
represents what the person meant. If it does not, it is a 0 and it is also an ethics
problem, not just a craft one.

## Thresholds

| Total | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 10–12 | Publish candidate |
| 9 | Publish candidate only if no single axis is below 2 |
| 7–8 | Rework list — name the axis holding it back |
| 0–6 | Leave it in the episode |

Raise the threshold, do not lower it, when volume is the problem. A weekly show that needs
four clips and produces eleven at 9+ should publish the top four, not all eleven. Publishing
the weak ones trains the account's distribution downward.

## Worked examples

### A. "We stopped hiring senior people entirely"

> **Guest:** We stopped hiring senior people entirely for about eighteen months. Everyone
> told us it was insane. But what we found was that a senior hire spends the first quarter
> arguing that the way they did it at their last company was better, and by the time they
> stop arguing, the thing they wanted to build has been built by someone with two years'
> experience who just did it.

Premise 3 (needs nothing), tension 3 (an actively contested hiring position), payoff 3
(the reasoning resolves inside), boundaries 3 (clean start, clean end). **12/12.** Shorts
and LinkedIn. Cold open on "We stopped hiring senior people entirely".

### B. "That's exactly the thing Dave was talking about"

> **Host:** Right, and that's exactly the thing Dave was talking about, isn't it? That
> whole dynamic. It's the same problem.

Disqualified on unresolved referent before scoring. It was a genuinely good moment in the
episode and it is not a clip. This is the most common false positive.

### C. The 40-second pricing walkthrough

> **Guest:** So the way we price it now — first, we work out what the customer saves in a
> year. Then we take a fifth of that. Then we sanity-check it against what the incumbent
> charges, and if we're above them we go back to step one. That's the whole method.

Premise 3, tension 1 (procedural, hard to argue with), payoff 3 (complete method),
boundaries 3. **10/12.** Publishes, but as a practical how-to rather than a hook clip.
Expect saves and shares rather than comments — the correct destination is LinkedIn or a
carousel-style Short, not a debate-bait Reel.

### D. The near-miss confession

> **Guest:** We were about six weeks from not making payroll. I hadn't told anyone on the
> team. My co-founder didn't know either, which — yeah, I would do that differently.

Premise 3, tension 2 (an admission, no opponent), payoff 2 (lands, but the trailing
self-correction needs trimming), boundaries 2 (one internal trim). **9/12**, no axis below
2, so it publishes. Out-point at "didn't know either" and cut the rest.
