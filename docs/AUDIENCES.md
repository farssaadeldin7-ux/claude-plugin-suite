# Audiences, required skill, and where the money is

Each plugin is matched to the audience with the most acute pain — measured in time lost or
revenue lost — and the budget to solve it. The "skill required" column is the thing the user
must already be good at for the plugin to be worth anything to them; it is also the honest
answer to "why doesn't this work for everyone".

## The five consumer-adjacent tools

| Plugin | Audience | Skill they must bring | Where the value lands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnose by Sound | Independent mechanics, workshop service advisers, serious DIY | Ability to run a road test and answer the discriminating questions honestly | Fastest immediate payback in the suite. Cuts diagnostic time before the car is on the lift, which raises bay throughput directly |
| Ghost Post Preview | Creators, social managers, small businesses | Content strategy — knowing which hooks are worth testing | Tied to revenue. Stops effort and paid spend going behind a post that was never going to convert |
| Professor Mind-Reader | University students, professionals writing to a rubric | Willingness to revise, and academic honesty | Premium subscription for students targeting the top band. Value is the grade delta, not the words |
| 5-Minute Fluency | Competitive players | Accurate self-report of what actually happens in their matches | The most scalable. On-demand coaching at a fraction of a human coach's cost, sold as a subscription |
| Basecamp Split | Outdoor guides, expedition leaders, trip organisers | Judgement about route and group competence, which the plugin explicitly does not provide | Growing guided-experience market. Reduces overhead and the forgotten-kit failure that ruins trips |

## Reading the pattern

Three things repeat across the list, and they are the reason to build these as skills rather
than as apps:

- **The pain is time inside an existing professional workflow**, not a new activity someone
  has to adopt. Nobody has to change what they do to get value.
- **Every one of them requires the user to already be competent.** These are amplifiers, not
  replacements, and the marketing should say so — it sets the right expectation and attracts
  the users who will renew.
- **The honest limits are a selling point to this audience.** Professionals distrust tools
  that never say "I don't know". Every plugin in the suite is built to say it.
